How to use this page

Built for how memory actually works

This isn't a wall of text to skim once. It's designed around a few well-studied ways the brain holds onto ideas — so a single pass sticks, and a five-minute revisit brings it all back.

Think in 6 buckets

30 loose facts are hard to hold. Six labelled layers — foundations, config, capability, orchestration, guardrails, observability — are easy. Learn the buckets first; the details hang off them.

Technique · Chunking

Peel, don't drown

Every concept opens the same way: one plain sentence, then an analogy, then why it matters, then the deep dive and resources. Stop at any layer. Come back for more when you're curious.

Technique · Progressive disclosure

One analogy each

Abstract ideas stick when they're tied to something you already know. Each concept ships with a single everyday picture — a controller, a rulebook, a coffee break — to anchor the memory.

Technique · Dual coding

Test before you trust

Reading feels like learning but rarely is. Every concept ends with one question — answer it in your head before you reveal the answer. That small effort is what moves it into long-term memory.

Technique · Retrieval practice

Mark what stuck

Tick a concept once it clicks. Your progress ring fills and saves in this browser — so you always know what to revisit next. Small wins keep curiosity alive.

Technique · Spaced revisits

Take it with you

Download any single concept — or the whole set — as a clean Markdown note. Drop it into your own notes, your team wiki, or your agent's MEMORY.md. Learning you can carry.

The mental model

Six layers, from the inside out

Picture an agent as a set of concentric rings. At the core, what it is. Around that, how you configure it, what it can reach for, how many of them work together, how you keep them safe, and finally how you watch what they did. Tap any layer to jump to its concepts.
